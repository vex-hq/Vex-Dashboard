## What changed

Everything your agent captured passively used to go straight into the shared
team brain. Now it lands in **your** private memory, and sharing is something
you do deliberately.

Nobody else can read your private scope. Not your teammates, and **not an
organisation administrator** — there is no override, no support path and no
break-glass. We would rather not ship the feature than ship it under a name
that isn't true.

## Where memories land now

| Scope | Who can read it | What lands there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Private** | Only you | Everything your editor captures passively |
| **Project** | Members of that project | Explicit writes made while a project is set |
| **Org** | Everyone on the team | Explicit writes — `remember`, `decide`, `plan`, `note` |

The rule is simple: **calling a write tool is the act of sharing.** Hooks record
what happened; they do not publish it.

A normal `recall` still searches all three in one call — your private memories,
your projects, and the shared brain. You do not have to think about where
something lives to find it.

## Promoting what turns out to matter

When a passive capture is worth the team seeing:

```json
{ "memory_id": "60a0fa87-…", "to": "org" }
```

`share` moves it and keeps you recorded as the author. Only the owner of a
memory can share it.

## The curator works for you now

Klio's curator reads your memories and distils them into higher-level facts. It
used to read the whole org and publish back to the whole org. It now runs **per
person**: it reads only your private captures and writes the results back into
your private scope, where they wait until you decide to share them.

Everything it produces is labelled `INFERRED` — an LLM's deduction, not
something you said. That label survives promotion, so a fact you share with the
team still carries the fact that it was deduced.

## Projects have real access control

Projects were a relevance filter. They can now be a boundary.

- `project_create` — name a project ahead of use
- `project_list` — what you can see
- `project_grant` — give a teammate access, **by email**
- `project_members` — who is in it

Every grant is recorded in an append-only audit trail: who, what, when, and
through which surface. A caller can only grant access to projects they
administer.

## In the dashboard

Three tabs, three separate queries:

- **Mine** — your private memories, and the curator's work on them
- **Projects** — pick a project, see its memories and artifacts
- **Team** — the shared brain

`INFERRED` memories render visibly differently from things you stated, so a
deduction never reads as a quote. There is a projects page with a members tab.
Administrators get every org and project memory plus a single org-wide storage
total — never the contents of a private scope, and never a per-person count of
it.

## What to expect on day one

**The shared brain will look smaller.** It drops to what people have
deliberately written, plus whatever gets promoted. That is the trade: tens of
thousands of rows of ambient session mechanics exchanged for a brain where
everything present was put there on purpose.

Existing memories are untouched. Nothing was retroactively hidden — every
memory written before today stays exactly as visible as it was.

Two consequences worth knowing. The curator no longer processes the existing
org-wide corpus, because it has no org-wide mode any more. And if an API key
was created without a recorded creator, writes through it cannot be attributed
to a person — those land at org scope, as they always did, and the curator
produces nothing for them.

## `agent` scope is deprecated

It resolved against a header your own client sends, so anyone could set it and
read someone else's notes. It was never a boundary. Existing `agent`-scoped
memories stay readable; nothing new is written there. Use private.
